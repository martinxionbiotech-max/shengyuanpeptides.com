# How to Compare Peptide Supplier Quotations

**Direct answer.** A quotation is comparable only after five things are aligned: Incoterms, content basis, purity basis, testing scope and payment terms. Until then, the lowest number is simply the quote that scoped least — and it will be the one that looks best in a spreadsheet.

Part of the [peptide supplier evaluation pillar](/knowledge-base/peptide-supplier-evaluation/).

## The five variables

| Variable | What to normalise | Typical spread |
|---|---|---|
| **Incoterms** | FOB / CIF / DDP — who pays freight, insurance, duty | 5–15% of order value |
| **Content basis** | Price per gram of powder vs per gram of peptide | 10–25% |
| **Purity basis** | ≥95% vs ≥98%, and by which method | 15–40% |
| **Testing scope** | In-house COA vs third-party report | 5–20% |
| **Payment terms** | 100% prepay vs 30/70 vs net 30 | Financing and risk, not a line item |

A quote that does not state all five is not yet a price. It is a starting position, and the negotiation will happen later, on the supplier's terms, when you are already committed.

## A worked comparison

Three quotes for 5 g of the same sequence. Numbers are illustrative.

| | Supplier A | Supplier B | Supplier C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline price | $1,200 | $1,560 | $1,380 |
| Incoterms | EXW | DDP | FOB |
| Purity | ≥95% (HPLC) | ≥98% (HPLC, method stated) | ≥98% (HPLC, method stated) |
| Content | not stated | 82% | 80% |
| Third-party test | not included | included | optional, +$180 |
| Payment | 100% prepay | 30/70 | 100% prepay |
| Freight + duty (est.) | +$210 | included | +$160 |
| Test (if added) | +$180 | — | +$180 |

**Landed cost for the same deliverable:**

- A: 1,200 + 210 + 180 = **$1,590** at 95% purity, content unknown
- B: **$1,560** at 98% purity, 82% content, tested
- C: 1,380 + 160 + 180 = **$1,720** at 98%, 80% content, tested

Supplier A looked 23% cheaper than B at the headline and is 2% more expensive once normalised — at a lower purity, with content unstated.

## Content basis: the error that hides in plain sight

If A's price is per gram of powder and B's is per gram of peptide, the comparison is already wrong:

```
5 g at 82% content = 4.1 g peptide
price per gram of peptide = 1,560 / 4.1 = $380/g
```

versus A, if per gram of powder at an assumed 80% content: `1,590 / 4.0 = $398/g`. The gap widens or narrows depending on an assumption you should not have to make. Ask for content.

## Hidden costs to add before comparing

- **Re-purification.** A batch at 92% against a 95% spec may need re-work; ask who pays and what the delay is.
- **Duty and VAT.** DDP quotes include them; FOB quotes do not. A 6–8% duty on a large order dwarfs a small price difference.
- **Your own testing.** If you plan to verify independently, add it to every column — not only the supplier you distrust.
- **Delay.** A three-week slip on a project timeline can exceed the entire price difference. Ask for the lead time in writing and treat slippage as a cost.
- **Payment timing.** 100% prepayment to a new supplier is an unsecured loan. Price the risk, or negotiate a deposit.

## Quote validity

Peptide prices move with resin, solvent and energy costs. A quote with no validity period is either a rough indication or a trap — if prices rise, the supplier re-quotes; if they fall, you have no mechanism to capture it.

Ask for validity in writing, typically 15–30 days for research quantities. A supplier who refuses a validity period is telling you the number is not firm.

## Building the comparison sheet

Six columns do the work:

1. Supplier and date
2. Normalised landed cost per gram of peptide
3. Purity with method
4. Content
5. Testing scope
6. Lead time and payment terms

Sort by column 2, then read columns 3–6 before deciding. The cheapest normalised cost with an unstated content or a 100% prepayment is not the cheapest option; it is the option with the most unquantified risk.

## Our position

**Headline price comparison is the most reliable way to select the weakest supplier.** It systematically rewards the quote that excludes testing, assumes the lowest purity and leaves content unstated. The buyer then discovers the gaps one at a time, each as a separate surprise.

**Normalising to cost per gram of peptide is the single most useful calculation in peptide procurement.** It converts three incomparable quotes into one number, and it forces the content question that most buyers never ask.

**Payment terms are part of the price and should be treated as such.** A 5% discount for 100% prepayment to a supplier you have not audited is not a discount; it is an unsecured position. Price the risk explicitly — even a rough 5–10% haircut for prepayment to an unknown counterparty is more honest than pretending the terms are free.

**A quote with no validity period should be treated as marketing.** It will change when it suits the supplier, and the change will arrive after you have planned around the number.
