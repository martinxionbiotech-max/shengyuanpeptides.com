# A Supplier Quality Documentation Checklist

**Direct answer.** Ask for four documents before you pay, check six things when they arrive, and verify three on delivery. If any of the four "stop" items fails, do not place the order — not because the supplier is necessarily dishonest, but because you will have no way to tell.

Part of the [peptide quality documentation pillar](/knowledge-base/peptide-quality-documentation/).

## Stage 1 — before you request a quote

- [ ] Can the supplier state the **sequence** of the product, including terminal modifications?
- [ ] Do they publish the **batch number** on the certificate, or only a product name?
- [ ] Is there a named **testing laboratory**, in-house or third-party?
- [ ] Will they quote a price for a **buyer-nominated third-party test**?

Four questions, five minutes. A supplier who cannot answer the second is a reseller of someone else's documents.

## Stage 2 — with the quotation

Request a **real certificate for a batch that exists**, not the sample PDF from the website.

| Document | Check | Stop if |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate of analysis | Batch number present and consistent | No batch number |
| | Purity figure names column, gradient, wavelength | "≥98% (HPLC)" only |
| | Measured mass stated with method | "Conforms", no value |
| | Peptide content reported | Absent *and* the answer is "we don't measure it" |
| Specification | Acceptance criteria per attribute | Three-line catalogue spec only |
| Method summary | Laboratory and method for each test | "In-house methods", no detail |
| Retention policy | Retention period for samples stated | No retained samples at all |

**Four stop items:** no batch number; no method for purity; no measured mass; no retained samples. Each removes your ability to resolve a dispute later.

## Stage 3 — when the goods arrive

- [ ] **Label batch number matches the certificate.** Photograph both before opening.
- [ ] **Dates are coherent** — manufacture ≤ test ≤ release, and release before shipment.
- [ ] **Vial weight is plausible** for the quantity ordered, allowing for content and counter-ion.
- [ ] **Appearance and solubility** match the certificate at the stated concentration.
- [ ] **Certificate is signed and dated**, not an unsigned template.
- [ ] **Packing list and invoice carry the same batch number.**

Any mismatch here is a traceability break, and traceability breaks are how substitutions survive.

## Stage 4 — if something fails

Ask three questions in this order:

1. **Can you send the batch record for this lot?** Not the certificate — the record behind it.
2. **Can you re-test the retained sample?** If there is no retained sample, this question answers itself.
3. **Which laboratory ran the original test, and can they confirm it directly?**

A supplier with a real process answers all three within a day. A supplier without one answers the first two with an explanation and the third with a copy of the same PDF.

## The scoring shortcut

If you want a number rather than a checklist, score the five dimensions in [how to evaluate a supplier's quality documents](/knowledge-base/evaluating-peptide-supplier-quality-documents/): identity, purity, content, traceability and laboratory independence, 0–3 each.

| Total | Reading |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Datasheet supplier; treat all claims as unverified |
| 6–9 | Typical market; budget for your own testing |
| 10–12 | Serious research supplier; documentation supports a sourcing decision |
| 13–15 | Approaching pharmaceutical-grade; verify the accreditation scope before believing it |

## What the checklist cannot do

It cannot tell you whether the material is any good — only whether the paperwork would let you find out. A supplier can pass every item and still ship a batch that fails your assay; the checklist ensures that when that happens, you have a batch number, a retained sample and a laboratory to go back to.

It also cannot substitute for your own testing on material that matters. The documents narrow the risk; they do not remove it.

## Our position

**The checklist is deliberately short.** Longer supplier questionnaires produce longer answers, not better ones — and the additional questions tend to be the ones a reseller can answer from a template. Four stop items and six arrival checks catch most of what goes wrong.

**"No retained samples" is the item buyers underweight.** It is the difference between a dispute you can resolve with a re-test and a dispute you resolve with an argument. Ask before ordering, not after.

**If a supplier fails a stop item, the right response is to walk, not to negotiate.** There is no discount that compensates for documentation that cannot support a technical decision. In a market where the buyer usually cannot test what arrives, the documents are the only leverage there is.
